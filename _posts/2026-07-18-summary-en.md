## [Chiral-Controlled TMDC Nanotube Synthesis Achieved, 30-Year Problem Solved](https://www.ithome.com/0/978/594.htm) ⭐️ 10.0/10

A team led by Zhejiang University achieved the first chiral-controlled synthesis of TMDC nanotubes, selectively producing armchair nanotubes, solving a 30-year challenge. The results were published in Science on July 17, 2025. This breakthrough enables the production of TMDC nanotubes with tailored electronic properties, crucial for nanoscale transistors and high-speed electronics, and opens a new direction for chiral-selective synthesis of nanomaterials. The team used boron nitride nanotubes as templates to guide the growth of TMDC nanotubes, achieving up to 84% selectivity for armchair-type tin disulfide nanotubes. The mechanism was confirmed through in-situ TEM observation and thermodynamic analysis.

Chirality, referring to the atomic arrangement along the tube, determines their electronic behavior. Controlled synthesis of specific chiralities has been a major challenge for over three decades.

## [Retrospective on Bikeshedding in Software Engineering](https://queue.acm.org/detail.cfm?id=3818307) ⭐️ 8.0/10

An ACM Queue article offers a retrospective on the concept of bikeshedding, providing practical advice for handling trivial decisions in software engineering. Bikeshedding wastes significant team time and energy; understanding it helps engineers prioritize effectively and improve productivity. The article discusses the origin of the term from Poul-Henning Kamp's 1999 email, and emphasizes that reversible decisions should be made quickly by the person doing the work.

hackernews · Ygg2 · Jul 18, 17:27 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48960155)

**Background**: Bikeshedding (also known as Parkinson's law of triviality) refers to the tendency for groups to spend disproportionate time on trivial issues while neglecting more important ones. The term originated from an anecdote about approving a nuclear power plant design vs. a bikeshed color. In software, it often manifests in lengthy debates over code style or naming conventions.

## [turbovec: Rust Vector Index Using Google's TurboQuant](https://github.com/RyanCodrai/turbovec) ⭐️ 8.0/10

turbovec is a new Rust vector index with Python bindings, built on Google Research's TurboQuant algorithm, achieving dramatic memory reduction from 31 GB to 4 GB for a 10 million document corpus while outperforming FAISS in search speed. This innovation makes large-scale vector search feasible on memory-constrained devices and reduces cloud costs significantly. It also simplifies deployment with online ingestion and no training step, benefiting RAG systems, privacy-sensitive applications, and edge computing. turbovec uses hand-written SIMD kernels (NEON and AVX-512BW) for fast search, supports online ingestion without retraining, and allows filtered search with an allowlist at query time. It is available on PyPI and crates.io.

rss · GitHub Trending - Daily · Jul 18, 01:33

**Background**: Vector search indexes enable similarity search over dense embeddings, crucial for applications like RAG. Traditional indexes use float32 vectors which are memory-heavy. Quantization reduces memory by using lower bit-width representations. TurboQuant is a data-oblivious quantizer that achieves near-optimal distortion without a separate training phase, making it ideal for dynamic datasets. turbovec is the first practical implementation of TurboQuant for vector search in Rust.

The proposal, led by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, is under review by White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles and has not yet been seen by President Trump. This initiative aims to address concerns from Wall Street about cybersecurity and Silicon Valley's frustrations with ad-hoc government restrictions on AI releases. If enacted, it could create a more structured, industry-influenced regulatory framework for AI safety in the United States. The proposed body would report to the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), similar to FINRA's structure. The plan aligns with a recent suggestion by Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis for an industry-funded independent AI regulator.
